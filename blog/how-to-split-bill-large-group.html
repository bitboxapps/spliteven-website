How to Split a Restaurant Bill for a Large Group (10+ People)

📅 3 September 2026 ⏱️ 5 min read ✍️ SplitEven Team
Quick Answer

For 10+ people, the cleanest approach is: one person pays the full bill, scans the receipt with a bill splitting app, and shares an itemised breakdown link in the group chat. Everyone sees exactly what they owe and can settle up immediately — no waiting, no chasing, no confusion at the register.

A birthday dinner for twelve. A farewell lunch for a colleague. A work team dinner. Any of these situations means one thing: a large, complicated bill at the end of the night, with varying orders, a growing tip question, and a table full of people trying to work out what to do.

Large group bills amplify every problem that exists with small group bills. The variance in orders is higher (some people had three courses and cocktails, some just had a main and water). The maths is more complex. The social pressure to just "split it evenly and move on" is stronger. And the injustice of that solution is larger — because at ten or twelve people, the gap between what someone ordered and what they're asked to pay can be very significant.

Here's how to handle it without turning the end of the evening into a maths lesson.

Before You Sit Down: Two Things to Sort

1. Tell the restaurant upfront

When making the reservation or as soon as you arrive, let the restaurant know how you plan to pay. If you want separate checks, ask now — not when the bill arrives. Many restaurants can accommodate separate checks when set up from the start but will struggle or refuse to split it after the fact. If you're planning to pay as one bill with one card and split it via app, there's nothing the restaurant needs to do.

Also ask about automatic gratuity. Most restaurants add 18–20% service for parties of 8 or more. Knowing this upfront removes the "how much should we tip?" debate at the end — the answer is already on the bill.

2. Decide on the split method

The two realistic options for large groups are an equal split (everyone pays the same) or an itemised split (everyone pays for what they ordered). Decide which one you're using before anyone orders — not after the bill arrives when opinions have formed based on people's own totals.

Equal splits work for large groups only when orders are genuinely similar. When there's a mix of light eaters, heavy drinkers, and people with entirely different starters and dessert habits, an equal split redistributes a meaningful amount of money. At ten people, the distortion can easily be £20–£40 per person in either direction.

The Recommended Method: One Card, App Scan, Group Link

This is the approach that works best for most large group dinners:

  1. One person pays the bill in full — on a single card. This keeps the transaction simple for the restaurant and avoids the server managing multiple cards for a large party. If you're the organiser or host, this is usually you.
  2. Scan the receipt immediately — open SplitEven and photograph the bill before leaving the table. The AI reads every item, price, and any automatic gratuity automatically.
  3. Assign items to people — tap each item and assign it to whoever ordered it. For shared starters or dishes, assign to everyone who shared it. For large groups, this takes a few minutes — but it's faster than trying to do mental arithmetic for twelve people.
  4. Share the breakdown link — SplitEven generates a shareable link with each person's full itemised total. Send it to the group chat. Every person opens it in their browser — no app needed — and sees exactly what they ordered and what they owe, including their proportional share of tax and the automatic gratuity.
  5. Collect payment — people pay whoever covered the bill via bank transfer, payment app, or cash. With the link in the group chat, everyone has a clear number and a reminder without you having to chase anyone individually.

The Tip Question — Settled Before It Becomes a Discussion

Tip debates at large group dinners are one of the most common sources of end-of-evening friction. Someone thinks 15% is enough. Someone else always tips 20%. Someone doesn't know whether the service charge was already included.

For groups of 8+, automatic gratuity eliminates this entirely. The tip is on the bill. It's already been calculated. There's nothing to decide. When you scan the receipt with SplitEven, the automatic gratuity is read as part of the total and divided proportionally — people who ordered more pay more of it, people who ordered less pay less.

For smaller groups where the tip isn't automatic, see our guide on how to calculate tip and tax when splitting a bill for the full breakdown of how to handle this fairly.

When Someone Ordered Significantly More or Less

At large groups, order variance is almost always significant. Twelve people rarely all have a main and a glass of wine. Some will have had starters, cocktails, dessert, and extra sides. Others will have had a modest single-course meal with tap water.

An even split at a table of twelve, where the total bill is £480, means everyone pays £40. But if four people had three-course meals with drinks totalling £60+ each, and the remaining eight had a main and one drink at £30 each, the even split overcharges eight people by £10 each and undercharges four people by £20 each. The itemised split produces a fair result for everyone — and at large groups, "everyone" means the difference matters more, not less.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the easiest way to split a restaurant bill between 10 or more people?
The easiest method is for one person to pay the entire bill on a single card, then use a bill splitting app to scan the receipt and share an itemised breakdown with the group immediately. Everyone sees exactly what they owe — including their share of tax and tip — and can pay the organiser directly. This is faster than managing multiple cards at the table and avoids the chaos of trying to split the payment with the restaurant.

Should you ask for separate checks at a large group dinner?
You can, but ask before you order — not when the bill arrives. Many restaurants can accommodate separate checks if set up in the system from the start, but they may decline or struggle to split it after the fact. For parties of 8 or more, some restaurants add an automatic gratuity to a single bill and may not be able to split it across many cards. Confirm the restaurant's policy when you make the reservation.

How do you handle automatic gratuity when splitting a large group bill?
Most restaurants add an automatic gratuity of 18–20% for parties of 8 or more. This is already included in the total, so you don't need to calculate or add a separate tip. When splitting the bill, the auto-gratuity is part of the total and divides proportionally with the rest. Check the bill carefully — if a gratuity has been added, don't double-tip on top of it.

What's the best app for splitting a large group restaurant bill?
SplitEven works well for large groups: scan the receipt, assign each item to whoever ordered it, and the app calculates each person's exact share including proportional tax and tip. Share the breakdown via a link — everyone opens it in their browser without downloading anything, sees their itemised total, and pays. For very large groups where orders varied significantly, the itemised method is the only approach that feels fair to everyone.

How do you split the bill fairly when some people in a large group ordered a lot more?
Use an itemised split rather than an even split. Scan the receipt and assign each item to whoever ordered it. People who had more courses, more drinks, or more expensive dishes pay proportionally more. People who ordered less pay proportionally less. An even split in a large group — where order variance tends to be highest — almost always means some people significantly overpay.
