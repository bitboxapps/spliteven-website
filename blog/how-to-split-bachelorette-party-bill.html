How to Split a Bachelorette or Bachelor Party Bill (Without the Drama)

📅 27 August 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read ✍️ SplitEven Team
Quick Answer

For meals and activities during the trip, use a bill splitting app to scan receipts and share breakdowns on the spot. The drama comes from vague expectations and delayed asks; fix both before anyone gets on a plane.

The average bachelorette party now costs each guest around £1,300. Bachelor party guests aren't far behind at £1,500. For a destination weekend with a large group, some guests are spending over £3,000. That's serious money — and serious potential for fallout when the financial expectations aren't clear.

Brides have been invoiced by their Maids of Honour. Guests have dropped out when budgets weren't discussed upfront. Friendships have been strained by vague contribution asks followed by months of attempted collection. None of this is inevitable. It comes from skipping one conversation — the money conversation — before the planning starts.

Here's how to handle the finances of a bachelorette or bachelor party so that everyone knows what they're paying, why, and when.

Who Pays for What — The Basics

The convention is consistent across most bachelorette and bachelor traditions: guests cover all costs, including the guest of honour's share. The Maid of Honour or Best Man organises everything but doesn't personally fund the event — they coordinate, collect, and execute.

Practically, this means each guest pays their own costs plus a proportional share of the bride's or groom's expenses. If there are eight guests and the bride's weekend share comes to £300, each of the eight guests pays an additional £37.50 on top of their own costs.

Travel is the standard exception. Guests pay for their own flights or long-distance transport, and the guest of honour pays for theirs. Shared transport at the destination (taxis, a party bus, a hired car) typically falls back into the shared costs.

The Budget Conversation — Have It Before You Plan Anything

The single most important thing the Maid of Honour or Best Man can do is ask each guest privately what budget they're comfortable with — before announcing any plans. Not in the group chat. Privately.

When people are asked publicly, they'll often say a higher number than they can actually afford because they don't want to seem like the person who limits the group's plans. When asked privately, they'll tell you the truth.

Build the event around the lowest comfortable budget. A weekend that everyone can genuinely afford and enjoy is better than a lavish itinerary that two people are silently stressed about. If someone's budget is genuinely incompatible with the group's plans, it's better to know that before booking than after.

Collect Money Upfront

The most effective way to avoid post-event collection headaches is to collect before the event happens. Once the itinerary is confirmed and the costs are known, send each guest a clear breakdown:

  • Their share of accommodation
  • Their share of pre-booked activities
  • Their share of the guest of honour's costs
  • A buffer for on-the-day incidentals (optional but useful)

Ask for payment within two weeks of sending the breakdown. The excitement of the upcoming event makes people act — trying to collect after the trip, when it's just a memory, is significantly harder.

Splitting On-the-Day Expenses

Pre-booked costs are the easier part. The trickier part is the organic, unplanned spending that happens during the event itself: the restaurant dinner, the bar tab, the extra round of drinks, the souvenir nobody planned for.

Meals

For group meals, one person pays the full bill and the others settle up immediately using a bill splitting app. Scan the receipt, assign items to each person, include the bride's share across everyone, and share the breakdown. Payment happens before anyone leaves the restaurant — no chasing required.

Drinks

The group bar tab is where costs can spiral unexpectedly. A few approaches: rotate rounds (each person buys a round for the group), use a tab that gets settled at the end of the night and split evenly, or pay individually at the bar. For groups with very different drinking levels, individual payment or assigning drinks by person (as you would with a receipt) is the fairest approach.

The bride's or groom's meals and drinks

Traditionally the group covers the guest of honour's food and drinks throughout the event. In practice, some groups cover everything; others cover meals but not every round of drinks. Decide this clearly before the event so nobody is surprised.

When Guests Have Different Budgets

This is the situation that generates most of the drama — and it's entirely avoidable with one upfront conversation.

If a guest tells you privately that the planned budget is beyond what they can manage, you have a few options: adjust the itinerary to bring costs down, offer a reduced participation (they join for some elements but not others), or let them opt out gracefully. None of these conversations are as difficult as they seem when they happen before the bookings are made.

What makes it difficult is when costs are presented as a fait accompli after plans are already locked in. At that point, the guest either accepts financial stress or becomes the person who backed out — neither is a good outcome. For Destination Bachelorettes and Bachelor Trips

When the event spans multiple days and includes accommodation, the same principles from our group trip expenses guide apply: collect upfront for accommodation and pre-booked activities, use a bill splitting app for all on-the-ground expenses, and do a final settlement at the end of the trip rather than trying to collect individual amounts throughout.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who pays for the bachelorette party?
The guests — bridesmaids, close friends, and anyone else invited — pay for the bachelorette party, including covering the bride's share. The Maid of Honour typically organises everything but doesn't pay for everyone individually. Each guest covers their own costs plus a proportional share of the bride's expenses. For bachelor parties, groomsmen follow the same convention for the groom.

Does the bride pay for her own bachelorette party?
By convention, no — the bride's costs are covered collectively by the guests. This includes her meals, accommodation, and activities. Travel costs (flights, long-distance transport) are usually the exception: guests typically pay for their own travel, and the bride pays for hers. The group should agree upfront on exactly what they're covering for the bride to avoid any surprises.

How do you fairly split costs when guests have different budgets?
Ask privately before planning. The Maid of Honour (or best man) should reach out to each guest individually to ask what budget they're comfortable with, before announcing any plans. Build the event around the lowest comfortable budget — it's better to have a smaller event where everyone can genuinely participate than a lavish one where some guests feel financially stressed or excluded.

What's the best way to collect money for a bachelorette party?
Collect upfront, before the event. Once the itinerary is set, send each guest a breakdown of what they owe and ask for payment within a week or two. For individual expenses during the event — meals, activities — use a bill splitting app to scan receipts and share itemised breakdowns instantly, so nobody needs to calculate or collect anything manually during the trip.

How do you split a bachelorette party bill when guests arrive at different times?
For shared accommodation, use the per-person per-night method: divide the accommodation cost by the number of nights, then split each night only among the guests who were there. For one-off costs (activities, group meals, the bride's share), split these equally among whoever participated. Keep a running log of expenses and share a final settlement at the end of the trip.
